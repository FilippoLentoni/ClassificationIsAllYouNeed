# Findings, limitations, costs and open items

Generated by `python -m ciayn report --mode live` from the saved outputs on 2026-10-02; every figure

## Findings

1. **On text, zero-shot Jev is accurate and close to calibrated.** Accuracy 0.960 on SST-2
   and 0.879 on AG News, with expected calibration error 0.020 and 0.082.
   A prior public evaluation of the same model version reports 0.964 and
   0.885.
2. **On the two tabular datasets its default decision fails.** Accuracy 0.467 on German
   Credit and 0.411 on Breast Cancer Wisconsin Diagnostic, against 0.741 and
   0.949 for supervised XGBoost. Jev calls 0.70 of applicants bad
   (true share 0.30) and 0.95 of masses malignant (true share
   0.37).
3. **The two tabular failures are different.** On Breast Cancer the probabilities rank well (ROC-AUC
   0.917); a threshold selected on 114 validation labels raises accuracy from
   0.405 to 0.735 and lowers the mean decision cost from
   0.605 to 0.395 (XGBoost: 0.147).
   On German Credit the ranking itself is weak (ROC-AUC 0.537) and no threshold helps much.
4. **XGBoost.** On the text datasets, XGBoost sees only TF-IDF features and reaches 0.791 on SST-2 and 0.914 on AG News (zero-shot Jev: 0.960 and 0.879). The learning curves (in the paper) put the comparison in terms of labels: on SST-2 it does not reach zero-shot Jev even with all 65,349 training labels; on AG News it first reaches zero-shot Jev at 28,752 training labels.
5. **Importance sampling guided by Jev's reported uncertainty beat uniform sampling on 1
   of 4 datasets.** Variance ratios at 400 labels (above 1 favours guided sampling):
   0.23 German Credit, 1.32 Breast Cancer,
   0.48 SST-2, 0.28 AG News. Jev rounds probabilities to two decimals, so many items
   have a reported uncertainty of exactly zero (5,621 of 7,600 on AG News),
   and the errors among them (307 of 923) are heavily weighted.
   The estimator itself is unbiased (largest absolute bias 0.005).
6. **Explanations run on Jev and are faithful on two datasets.** Deleting the three top KernelSHAP
   units lowers the predicted-class probability by 0.577 (German Credit) and
   0.401 (SST-2), against 0.008 and 0.096 for
   random units.
   They are weak on Breast Cancer (0.065) and AG News (0.075).
   With every feature removed, Jev still gives "bad" a probability of 0.69 on
   German Credit.
7. **Fine-tuned BERT overtakes both zero-shot models on AG News but not on SST-2.** Macro-F1
   0.942 on AG News (Jev: 0.878); it first matches Jev at
   5,752 training labels. On SST-2 it reaches 0.916 with all
   65,349 labels, below Jev (0.960). On serialized tabular rows BERT is weak
   (0.473 on German Credit, 0.716 on Breast Cancer); XGBoost stays the best
   tabular model.
8. **Self-hosted Qwen3.6-27B matches Jev on text and avoids its bias on Breast Cancer.** Macro-F1
   0.956 on SST-2 and 0.877 on AG News (Jev: 0.960 and
   0.878); 0.783 on Breast Cancer (Jev: 0.343) and
   0.457 on German Credit (Jev: 0.467). It cost USD 0.352 to
   1.342 per 1,000 predictions on ml.g6.12xlarge, 24 to
   44 times Jev's USD 0.015 to 0.030, and took
   0.92 to 1.10 s for a single item against 0.15 s for a Jev
   request. With thinking enabled, on 50 items per dataset, its macro-F1 rose on every
   dataset (for example 0.772 to 0.890 on Breast Cancer).
9. **Escalating Jev's low-confidence predictions to Qwen helps on one dataset.** On Breast Cancer the
   ensemble reaches macro-F1 0.856, above Jev (0.343) and Qwen (0.783),
   escalating 0.69 of the items at USD 0.950 per 1,000 predictions. On German
   Credit it reaches 0.478; on SST-2 the selected threshold escalates nothing; on AG News
   0.04 of the items are escalated with no gain (0.878), and
   307 of Jev's 923 errors there carry its highest reported
   confidence, so no threshold can reach them.

## Limitations

- Jev and Qwen are zero-shot, XGBoost and BERT are supervised; the comparison is unequal by design, and on
  text XGBoost sees only TF-IDF features.
- Qwen's probabilities come from scoring option names and
  carry numerical noise at the second decimal; its cost and latency describe one deployment
  (ml.g6.12xlarge, batch job). Thinking was measured on 50 items per dataset.
- One frozen template per dataset, never tuned. The tabular templates were not tried on data rows.
  A public repository reports 0.842 zero-shot on Breast Cancer with a different
  serialization and prompt; we did not measure that sensitivity.
- No recalibration of any model.
- The tabular datasets are small; standard deviations over repeated splits describe split variation.
- Explanations cover 20 examples per dataset with 200 KernelSHAP coalitions.
- Only German Credit has a canonical cost matrix; the other two cost matrices are assumptions.
- Prior-work numbers were read from their sources through an automated web reader on 2026-09-30
  and stored with the quoted text; a person has not re-read the sources.

## Actual costs

- **Jev:** 50,000 requests of a cap of 60,000 (13,620 predictions,
  35,317 explanations, 1,000 escalation validation items,
  60 template checks, 3 connectivity checks); 0 failed. 39.0 million input tokens, USD 1.64, computed
  from recorded token counts. 1 request has no recorded token count.
- **AWS:** about USD 25.67 at list prices for all SageMaker jobs (limit USD 75),
  counting stopped, failed and repeated executions: USD 0.67 for XGBoost (12
  executions on ml.m5.xlarge), USD 1.97 for BERT (5 executions,
  1.57 GPU hours on ml.g5.xlarge) and USD 23.02 for Qwen
  (14 executions, 3.75 GPU hours). This is computed from job records, not
  from the AWS bill. Thinking on every item was projected at USD 49 and was
  not run.
- **Removed dataset:** Banking77 was run and then removed from the study. The totals above still
  include it: 9,198 Jev requests (USD 0.89), USD 1.05 of
  SageMaker jobs dedicated to it, and part of the USD 12.77 spent on
  9 exploratory Qwen executions that also covered other datasets.

## Incomplete or different from the plan

- No git commits were made (the user's decision). The freeze of the evaluation identifiers is evidenced by the hash manifest and its timestamp, not by a commit; a file snapshot of the earlier 100-inference project is kept outside the working copy.
- The first 100 Jev requests predate request logging. 99 regenerated requests hash to the stored cache keys; their raw responses and the first connectivity check were never recorded, so one request has no token count and the reported cost covers all requests but that one.
- Repeated splits of the tabular datasets do not make every row a test row (885 of 1,000 German Credit rows and 506 of 569 breast-cancer rows are in at least one test part). Jev predicts every row.
- For the two tabular datasets, a row that is a test row in one repeat is a training row in another, so its label is uploaded for that other repeat. Each repeat is fitted without its own test labels; for the text datasets, evaluation labels never left this machine.
- Text baselines use 16 histogram bins, chosen after the first AG News run was stopped for running too long. SST-2 was rerun with the same setting; the stopped and superseded runs are counted in the AWS cost.
- The local process watching the second AG News execution was killed when this machine ran out of memory. Its attempt record was written afterwards from SageMaker's own execution and training-job records (marked recorded_from in results/live/baseline_attempts.json); the job was not rerun.
- The confidence-guided importance sampler was kept as pre-specified (alpha = 1); it is not the variance-optimal choice for a 0/1 loss, and the paper reports alpha = 0.5 as a sensitivity result only.
- Qwen's inference ran in SageMaker training jobs, not processing jobs: every GPU quota for processing jobs was 0 in the three regions checked.
- Qwen's scoring method changed twice after measurements. vLLM with prompt log-probabilities recomputes the prompt for every option; vLLM's prefix cache works in 784-token blocks for this architecture, so per-edge requests did not help either. The final scoring uses transformers (prompt state reused across options, checked against a full recomputation on one item per dataset); vLLM only generates the reasoning of the thinking subsample.
- Thinking mode covers 50 seeded evaluation items per dataset with at most 2,048 reasoning tokens, reduced from the planned 100 items and 4,096 tokens after the first smoke run showed that only about 12 items reason concurrently on 24 GB GPUs.
- ml.g6e.12xlarge, the planned instance type, never had capacity in us-east-2; the Qwen jobs ran on ml.g6.12xlarge. Smoke runs and stopped executions needed to find a working method cost about USD 12 of the AWS total.
- XGBoost was run again on every dataset to record its inference time; its predictions are identical to the first runs (results/live/xgb_rerun_check.json).
- BERT's learning rate and number of epochs are selected on the first split repeat of the tabular datasets and reused for the other nine.
- Banking77 was run and then removed from the study on 2026-10-01 (the user's decision), because Qwen3.6-27B could not be run on it within the compute limit. Its results, split file and loader were deleted; the frozen split files of the four remaining datasets are byte-identical to the freeze (results/splits/REMOVED.json). What was spent on it is recorded in results/live/removed_datasets.json and stays in every total.
- 1,000 Jev requests were added for the escalation threshold (500 validation items each of AG News and Banking77; the user's decision). The 500 on Banking77 are unused.
- The paper was reformatted for NeurIPS 2026 as an anonymous submission. The detailed tables, figures and protocol text moved from the main text to appendices; nothing reported for the four remaining datasets was dropped. The NeurIPS paper checklist was written and then removed from the paper at the author's request (2026-10-02); NeurIPS requires it for a submission, and it is kept in paper/sections/checklist.tex.
- The AWS resources (one S3 bucket with the Qwen weights, one role, one policy in us-east-2) are still deployed; teardown waits for the user's confirmation.
